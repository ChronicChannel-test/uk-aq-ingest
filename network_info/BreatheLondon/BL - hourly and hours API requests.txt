Good question. They look similar on the surface, but they serve different purposes in the Breathe London Communities API.

### `/v3/sensors/{sensors_id}/measurements/hourly`

**What it returns**

* Actual **air quality measurements**
* Each record includes:

  * Timestamp (hourly)
  * Pollutant (for example PM2.5, PM10, NO2 if available)
  * Measured value
  * Units

**What it’s for**

* This is the endpoint you use when you want the **data itself**
* Plotting charts
* Calculating averages
* Ingesting observations into your database

**Think of it as**

> “Give me the hourly readings from this sensor”

---

### `/v3/sensors/{sensors_id}/hours`

**What it returns**

* A list of **hours for which data exists**
* Typically just timestamps or hour identifiers
* No pollutant values

**What it’s for**

* **Indexing and discovery**
* Checking data availability before fetching measurements
* Efficient backfilling logic

**Think of it as**

> “Tell me which hourly time slots have data for this sensor”

---

### Why both exist

The split is deliberate and useful:

* `/hours`

  * Lightweight
  * Fast
  * Lets you see coverage and gaps
* `/measurements/hourly`

  * Heavier
  * Returns full measurement payloads

This is especially helpful if you are:

* Backfilling historical data
* Respecting rate limits
* Avoiding repeated downloads of empty or already-ingested hours

---

### Typical ingestion pattern (recommended)

1. Call
   `/v3/sensors/{sensors_id}/hours`
   to see which hours exist
2. Compare with what you already have in your DB
3. Fetch only the missing hours via
   `/v3/sensors/{sensors_id}/measurements/hourly`

---

### One-line summary

* **`/hours` = availability metadata**
* **`/measurements/hourly` = the actual air quality data**
